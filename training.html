Chapter 01

It starts with a cohort and ends with a certificate.

Every intake is different: students, career-changers, home bakers formalising what they already know. What never changes is the send-off, a group photo with certificates in hand on the last day.

That's the goal for every cohort: not just a class finished, but a room full of people who can now do this for themselves.

Chapter 02

Hands-on from day one, no long lecture before the kitchen.

Training starts with pastries and snacks like puff-puff and chin chin: the fundamentals of frying, timing and finishing that everything else builds on.

It's also where the room becomes a team. By the second batch, trainees are checking each other's work before an instructor has to.

Chapter 03

Then cake artistry, the part everyone's waiting for.

Buttercream work, fondant character toppers and edible photo prints. This is where a trainee's cakes start to look like something you would actually order.

Character cakes teach precision piping and clean fondant work in a shape that hides no mistakes.

Chapter 04

Specialty builds, because real orders are never generic.

Football cakes for the fans, tall tiered builds for traditional ceremonies. Trainees leave able to take on a client's brief, not just repeat a template.

These are the projects that end up in a trainee's own portfolio, and often become their first paid order.

Chapter 05

Small enough that no one just watches.

Sessions run around a single table, close enough for an instructor to correct a piping angle mid-stroke.

It's slower than a lecture hall, and it's why the finished cakes look like the photos, not like a rough first attempt.

Chapter 06

The part that doesn't make it into a syllabus.

The technical skills are the point of the class. The reason people come back is the room itself: relaxed, hands-on, and full of people who care whether your cake turns out right.
